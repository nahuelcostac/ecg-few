\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

This Bachelor's Thesis studies automatic ECG analysis when the electrocardiogram is available as an image rather than as the original time series. The case study is suspected Brugada syndrome, a rare disease for which large labelled cohorts are difficult to assemble. The goal is to compare ways of using a small number of examples to prioritise ECGs for specialist review.

Two approaches are compared: a convolutional network trained under supervision and a vision-language model using in-context learning. Both receive one V1 image and are evaluated at patient level for \(K=\{2,4,8,16,32\}\). The experiments combine a QRS/ST simulator with 100 patients and a processed cohort from the Hospital Universitario Central de Asturias (HUCA) with 317 patients.

The ResNet18 learns three morphological findings from simulator patients. Its HUCA evaluation measures synthetic-to-real transfer because the cohort lacks independent RBBB, ST-elevation and T-wave-inversion annotations for training the multi-label head.

Gemma~4 uses labelled examples as prompt demonstrations without modifying its weights. Zero-shot, standard ICL with minimum coverage of both classes, and balanced ICL are compared using synthetic or clinical context.

On the simulator, the CNN reaches a Balanced Accuracy (BA) of \(0.885 \pm 0.013\) at \(K=32\), while balanced ICL reaches \(0.904 \pm 0.011\), compared with 0.500 in zero-shot. On HUCA, the CNN peaks at \(0.551 \pm 0.034\) for \(K=4\). MMD raises the \(K=32\) point to \(0.546 \pm 0.011\) without surpassing that maximum. Gemma~4 with synthetic demonstrations remains close to a constant negative prediction and reaches \(0.514 \pm 0.008\) at \(K=16\). With demonstrations from the HUCA cohort, balanced ICL reaches \(0.761 \pm 0.011\) at \(K=32\), with 0.741 sensitivity and 0.781 specificity.

Both approaches learn the synthetic rule, but transfer based on simulator examples remains limited. In-context learning performs best when demonstrations represent the local clinical task. Neither model has sufficient evidence for reliable clinical triage. The main contribution is to quantify this gap through a documented protocol and to identify the need for clinical morphology annotations, more positive cases, and external validation.

\textbf{Keywords:} ECG as image, Brugada syndrome, few labelled data, supervised learning, convolutional neural networks, vision-language models, in-context learning, clinical triage.
