\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

This Bachelor's Thesis studies the automatic analysis of electrocardiograms represented as images. It focuses on suspected Brugada syndrome, a rare disease for which large cohorts of labelled patients are difficult to assemble. The aim is to investigate how a small number of examples can be used to identify relevant patterns in an ECG trace.

Two strategies are compared. The first uses a supervised ResNet18 convolutional neural network. The second uses the Gemma~4 vision-language model through in-context learning, adding labelled examples to the prompt without modifying the model's weights. Both methods receive an image of lead V1 and are evaluated with sets of between 2 and 32 labelled patients. The experiments use a controlled domain comprising 100 patients generated with a QRS/ST simulator and a processed clinical cohort of 317 patients from Hospital Universitario Central de Asturias (HUCA).

In the synthetic domain, both methods identify the combination of morphological features defined for the experiment. With 32 examples, ResNet18 reaches a Balanced Accuracy (BA) of 0.885, while Gemma~4 reaches 0.904 when the demonstrations are selected to give both classes equal representation. These results, however, do not carry over to clinical ECGs. The CNN trained on synthetic data reaches a maximum BA of 0.551 on the HUCA cohort. Gemma~4 reaches 0.514 when its demonstrations come from the simulator and tends to classify cases as negative. Its behaviour changes when clinical demonstrations are selected within the validation procedure: with 32 examples, split equally between the two classes, Gemma~4 reaches a BA of 0.761, with 0.741 sensitivity and 0.781 specificity.

The results show a clear gap between synthetic and clinical ECGs. The simulator provides a useful environment for testing whether the models recognise a known combination of visual features. Its representation excludes part of the variability and the diagnostic reference found in real data. In-context learning performs better when the demonstrations come from the same domain as the evaluated cases. The available evidence remains insufficient to support a reliable clinical triage system. Further progress will require clinical morphology annotations, a larger number of positive cases, and external validation using data from other centres.

\textbf{Keywords:} ECG as image, Brugada syndrome, few-shot learning, convolutional neural networks, vision-language models, in-context learning, domain adaptation.
