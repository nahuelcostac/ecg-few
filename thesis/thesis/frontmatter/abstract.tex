\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

The scarcity of labelled examples makes it difficult to develop models for analysing electrocardiograms (ECGs) of rare diseases. This Bachelor's Thesis investigates how a few cases can support ECG image classification, using suspected Brugada syndrome as a case study. It compares supervised training of a ResNet18 convolutional neural network with in-context learning using Gemma~4, a vision-language model that receives labelled examples in its query without updating its weights.

Evaluation proceeds in two stages, first using 100 cases from the QRS/ST simulator and then recordings from 317 patients at Hospital Universitario Central de Asturias (HUCA). Both approaches analyse images of one heartbeat from the precordial lead V1, selected near the centre of each clinical recording. Experiments examine the effect of the number of available examples on recognising three waveform features defined in the simulator and classifying patients using HUCA clinical labels.

In the simulated setting, both methods improve as more examples become available and achieve similar performance. However, little of this performance transfers to real ECGs when learning relies on simulator cases, even with the domain adaptation variants studied. The difference between settings shows that recognising simulator feature combinations is insufficient to solve the clinical classification task.

With HUCA examples, Gemma~4 outperforms the CNN from eight demonstrations onwards and reaches a balanced accuracy of 0.761 with 32 cases equally divided between classes. The CNN trained on these data remains near chance, whereas Gemma~4 without demonstrations is unable to identify positive cases in this evaluation. The main finding is therefore the importance of the origin and relevance of the examples, alongside their number.

These results motivate further investigation of in-context learning to prioritise the review of suspicious ECGs and facilitate the collection of data for developing models with better performance or greater explainability. Such support would require clinical validation and would not replace a complete medical assessment.

\textbf{Keywords:} electrocardiogram as image, Brugada syndrome, few-shot learning, convolutional neural networks, vision-language models, in-context learning, domain adaptation.
